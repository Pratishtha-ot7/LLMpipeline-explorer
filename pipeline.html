What's happening in Step 1: Tokenization & Vocabulary

Computers cannot read letters directly—they only understand numbers. First, a language model breaks raw text into clean units called tokens, removes duplicates, and builds a numbered lookup dictionary (a vocabulary) so every known token has a unique ID.

What's happening in Step 2: Encoding & Decoding

Now that we have a vocabulary, we can translate any sentence into a sequence of integer IDs (Encoding). If a word wasn't in our Step 1 vocabulary, it gets mapped to [UNK] (ID 1)—showing why models with small vocabularies lose information about rare words!

What's happening in Step 3: Word Embeddings

A single token ID like 4 vs 5 is just an arbitrary label—ID 4 isn't "twice as royal" as ID 2. To capture properties and relationships, the model replaces each ID with a list of numbers called an embedding vector, where similar tokens get similar numbers.

What's happening in Step 4: Visualizing Embedding Space

Our embeddings have 6 dimensions, which humans can't easily picture. Using PCA (Principal Component Analysis), we project those 6D vectors onto a flat 2D map. Points close together are treated as similar by the model.
